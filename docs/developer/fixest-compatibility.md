# Fixest compatibility

R `fixest` is the default behavioral reference for overlapping pyfixest
features. Match its user-facing names, defaults, formula behavior, estimates,
and inference unless there is a documented reason not to.

An intentional difference is complete only when it has:

- a precise statement of both behaviors;
- a user or architectural rationale;
- an external reference version;
- permanent tests for the chosen pyfixest behavior and the observed difference;
- human-maintainer review.

Newly observed discrepancies are not automatically intentional. Open or link an
issue and investigate them before adding them to this ledger.

## Compatibility ledger

| Area | Pyfixest behavior | `fixest` behavior | Rationale | Tests | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Redundant endogenous terms | Deduplicates identical endogenous terms: `Y ~ X2 + [X2 ~ Z1]` is equivalent to `Y ~ [X2 ~ Z1]`. Multiple estimation steps that differ only by repeating the endogenous term, as in `Y ~ csw(X1, X2) \| X2 ~ Z1`, parse to the same model; one model is returned with a warning. Distinct transformed control terms may share source columns with endogenous variables; acceptance depends on numerical first-stage checks, as in `fixest`. | `fixest` 0.14.0 rejects `Y ~ X2 \| X2 ~ Z1` because the endogenous variable also appears among the exogenous regressors, and rejects stepwise steps that repeat it for the same reason. `fixest` 0.14.2 accepts distinct terms such as `Y ~ X2 \| X2:Z2 ~ Z1`. | Applies ordinary formula term deduplication to IV declarations. | `tests/test_formula_parse.py::TestEdgeCases::test_iv_semantic_deduplication`; `tests/test_iv.py::test_iv_generated_name_collision_against_fixest` compares redundant declarations against simplified live-R specifications; `tests/test_iv.py::test_iv_transformed_terms_against_fixest` covers valid transformed controls; `tests/test_formula_parse.py::test_duplicate_stepwise_models_warn` covers the stepwise warning. | Intentional; pending maintainer review |
| Constant instrument without intercept | In a model without intercept, controls, or fixed effects, such as `Y ~ 0 + [D ~ K]` with constant `K`, compares the first-stage SSR against the uncentered SSR of `D` and estimates the model. | `fixest` 0.14.0 compares against the SSR of `D` around its mean and raises "The instruments have 0 explanatory power". | Without an intercept, a constant instrument explains the mean of the endogenous variable; the centered SSR treats the model as if it had an intercept. Every other first-stage identification check uses the same thresholds and comparison as `fixest`. | `tests/test_errors.py::test_iv_constant_instrument_without_intercept` checks the estimate against the closed form `sum(Y) / sum(D)`; the R behavior was confirmed during PR #1992 review. | Intentional; pending maintainer review |
| Covariate powers | Follows Formulaic: outside Python expressions such as `I(...)`, `^` and `**` expand interactions. `X1 + X1^2` reduces to `X1`; `(X1 + X2)^2` expands to `X1 + X2 + X1:X2`. Use `I(X1**2)` for arithmetic squares, including in models with fixed effects. | `fixest` 0.14.0 rewrites covariate `X1^2` to `I(X1^2)` and `(X1 + X2)^2` to `I((X1 + X2)^2)`. | Prioritizes Formulaic's Wilkinson formula conventions over fixest's arithmetic-power convention; see [#1765](https://github.com/py-econometrics/pyfixest/issues/1765) and the [user-facing explanation](../explanation/compare-fixest-pyfixest.qmd#covariate-powers). | `tests/test_formula_parse.py::TestFixedEffectInteractions::test_formulaic_power_operators_match_outside_fixed_effects`; Formulaic 1.2.1 and `fixest` 0.14.0 observations recorded in #1765 | Intentional; documented for 0.70.0 |
| Stepwise grouping | Parenthesizes each stepwise replacement so surrounding formula operators apply to the entire step. | `fixest` 0.14.0 rejects stepwise calls combined with other operators. | Allows cumulative interactions without silently changing the requested model; see [#1774](https://github.com/py-econometrics/pyfixest/issues/1774). | `tests/test_formula_parse.py::TestMultipleEstimationExpansion::test_expand_all_multiple_estimation`; `tests/test_formula_parse.py::test_correct_number_of_models` verifies that all expanded models are fitted. | Intentional extension for 0.70.0 |
| Gaussian GLM inference | `feglm(family="gaussian")` matches `feols()`, base R `lm`, base R `glm`, and `fixest::feols` for OLS behavior and small-sample corrections. | `fixest::feglm(family="gaussian")` applies GLM small-sample corrections that differ slightly from `fixest::feols`. | A Gaussian identity-link model should agree with pyfixest OLS and base R's Gaussian linear-model behavior. | `tests/test_vs_fixest.py::test_feglm_gaussian_reference_behavior`; `tests/test_varying_slopes_vs_fixest.py::test_varying_slopes_glm_against_fixest` compares Gaussian slope models with `fixest::feols`; confirmed with R 4.5.3 and `fixest` 0.14.0 on 2026-08-25 | Intentional; documented for 0.70.0 |
| Clustering on interactions | `vcov={"CRV1": "f1^f2"}` raises `ValueError`; users add the interacted variable as a data column and cluster on it. | `cluster = ~f1^f2` clusters on the interaction of `f1` and `f2`. | Cluster variables are read from the data by name, and interacted fixed-effect columns are not materialized there. Earlier versions silently rewrote `^` to `_` and then failed on the missing column. | `tests/test_errors.py::test_vcov_spec_rejects_malformed_input`; `fixest` 0.14.0 behavior recorded 2026-09-18 | Unsupported; documented for 0.70.0 |
| Covariance eigenvalue correction | Leaves indefinite covariance estimates uncorrected. Negative diagonal entries yield `NaN` standard errors; positive diagonals do not guarantee valid joint inference. | `fixest` 0.14.0 defaults to `vcov_fix = TRUE`: when eigenvalues are nonpositive, it floors eigenvalues at `1e-16` and warns if any matrix entry changes by more than `1e-8`. | Observed implementation gap, not an intentional statistical choice; see the [user-facing note](../explanation/compare-fixest-pyfixest.qmd#covariance-eigenvalue-correction). | Confirmed from installed `fixest` 0.14.0 on 2026-10-05. The fixture in `tests/test_multiway_clustering_vs_fixest.py` uses positive-definite covariances and does not cover repair; a permanent indefinite-covariance regression is still needed. | Unresolved compatibility gap |
| Empty first interaction cell | With an intercept or fixed effects, `i(a, b)` without explicit references may omit a different observed cell and warn about collinearity. | `fixest` 0.14.0 uses the first observed cell. | Accepted: equivalent fitted model and inference after aligning the reference; see [#1768](https://github.com/py-econometrics/pyfixest/issues/1768). | — | Intentional; documented for 0.70.0 |
| `i()` levels in split samples | With `split` or `fsplit`, selects levels and validates `ref` and `ref2` separately within each sample, before missing-row filtering. An explicit reference absent from any split sample raises `ValueError`, even if present elsewhere in the supplied data. | `fixest` 0.14.0 determines levels from the full supplied data before splitting. A reference present in the full data remains accepted when absent from a subsample; collinear columns are removed during that fit. | Each split fit uses only levels observed in its own sample, consistently for string and pandas categorical columns. Default-reference fits can have different coefficient representations while giving equivalent fitted values. See [#1912](https://github.com/py-econometrics/pyfixest/pull/1912). | `tests/test_i.py::test_split_levels_choose_sample_reference` and `::test_split_reference_validity` compare default references, predictions, and explicit reference validity for string and categorical columns with live `fixest` 0.14.0. | Intentional; documented for 0.70.0 |
| Mixed-case string levels | String levels are sorted by code point, so uppercase sorts before lowercase: with levels `apple`, `Banana`, `cherry`, `i(m)` and `C(m)` use `Banana` as the reference. | `fixest` 0.14.0 sorts levels by R's collation locale: under `en_US.UTF-8` the reference is `apple`; under the `C` locale it matches pyfixest. | Code-point order is deterministic and locale-independent; `i(m, ref="apple")` reproduces the `en_US.UTF-8` result. See [#1802](https://github.com/py-econometrics/pyfixest/issues/1802). | Confirmed with R 4.5.3 and `fixest` 0.14.0 on 2026-10-04 | Intentional; documented for 0.70.0 |
| `i(a)*X` slopes | Follows formulaic's `C(a)*X` convention: `X` is the slope of the reference level, and `a::a2:X`, `a::a3:X` are differences from it. | `fixest` 0.14.0 drops the last interaction `X:a::a3` as collinear, so `X` is the slope of the last level, and `X:a::a1`, `X:a::a2` are differences from it. | Same fitted model; changing it would need custom scoping rules inside formulaic. `i(a) + X + i(a, X, ref="a3")` reproduces fixest's coefficients, and `i(a) + i(a, X)` gives one slope per level in both packages. See [#1803](https://github.com/py-econometrics/pyfixest/issues/1803). | Confirmed with R 4.5.3 and `fixest` 0.14.0 on 2026-10-04 | Intentional; documented for 0.70.0 |

| Fixed-effect dtypes | Rejects `object` FE columns during fitting and prediction; prediction requires the original fitted dtype, including the categorical schema. | `fixest` 0.14.0 matches prediction values against fitted level names; an integer FE accepts `1`, `1.0`, and `"1"`. | Explicit dtypes prevent ambiguous object labels and implicit cross-dtype matching; users choose conversions. | `tests/test_formulaic_compat.py::test_fe_prediction_requires_fitted_dtype`, `::test_fe_rejects_object_dtype`; R behavior recorded in [#1900](https://github.com/py-econometrics/pyfixest/pull/1900) | Intentional; pending maintainer review |
| Boolean varying slopes | `f1[b]` with a boolean `b` is accepted and treated as its 0/1 coding, giving the same fit as a float column. | `fixest` 0.14.0 rejects it: "variables with varying slopes must be numeric". | Slopes are evaluated as formula expressions like any other regressor, which already code booleans as 0/1; rejecting them would need dtype inspection of arbitrary slope expressions. | `tests/test_varying_slopes.py::test_varying_slopes_boolean_slope_equals_zero_one_slope`; R behavior checked with R 4.5.3 and `fixest` 0.14.0 on 2026-10-26 | Intentional; pending maintainer review |
| Separation in slope-only effects | With `separation_check=["fe", "ir"]`, a slope-only group such as `g[[z]]` with all-zero outcomes and a one-signed `z` is dropped as separated, because its slope can send every fitted mean to zero. | `fixest` 0.14.0 keeps the group. | The group has no finite maximum-likelihood estimate; dropping it leaves the other coefficients unchanged but removes its observations from `n_obs` and from the standard errors. | `tests/test_varying_slopes_vs_fixest.py::test_varying_slopes_one_signed_separation_differs_from_fixest` (120 observations in `fixest`, 100 in pyfixest; coefficients agree, standard errors differ); R 4.5.3 and `fixest` 0.14.0 on 2026-10-26 | Intentional; pending maintainer review |
| Slope-only effects in multiple estimation | Each model of `Y ~ X1 \| sw(f1[X2], f1[[X2]])` equals its single fit: the slope-only model keeps and reports an `Intercept`. | `fixest` 0.14.0 multiple estimation fits the slope-only model without an intercept, although the single fit `Y ~ X1 \| f1[[X2]]` keeps it; the `X1` estimates differ. | A multiple estimation is a shorthand for its single fits and must not change a model; tracked in [#2003](https://github.com/py-econometrics/pyfixest/issues/2003). | `tests/test_varying_slopes_vs_fixest.py::test_varying_slopes_multiple_estimation_against_fixest` compares each model with its single `fixest` fit, and `::test_fixest_multiple_estimation_drops_slope_only_intercept` asserts the observed difference; `tests/test_vs_fixest.py::test_multi_fit` covers only slope models with intercepts. R 4.5.3 and `fixest` 0.14.0 on 2026-10-26 | Intentional; pending maintainer review |
| Slope-only effects in IV with an intercept | `Y ~ X2 + [X1 ~ Z1] \| f1[[Z2]]` matches explicit dummy-variable 2SLS in both stages: the intercept counts in `df_k`, and residuals and standard errors include it. | `fixest` 0.14.0 estimates the same coefficients but counts one parameter fewer in both stages and reports different second-stage residuals and standard errors in both stages. The `0 +` model and the OLS analogue agree with pyfixest. | Coefficients, residuals, and inference should describe the same 2SLS model; tracked in [#2004](https://github.com/py-econometrics/pyfixest/issues/2004). | `tests/test_varying_slopes_vs_fixest.py::test_varying_slopes_iv_intercept_differs_from_fixest` compares with explicit 2SLS and asserts the observed `fixest` difference; R 4.5.3 and `fixest` 0.14.0 on 2026-10-26 | Unresolved `fixest` discrepancy; pending maintainer review |

## Adding an entry

Add an entry in the same PR that introduces or formalizes the difference. Name
the exact external package version in the test or its reference artifact. Link
the issue or decision record when the rationale is too large for the table.

Compatibility work that restores parity should update or remove the ledger
entry and retain a regression test.
